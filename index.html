The Science of Sleep

Understanding Your Body's Nightly Maintenance Program

Sleep is not just "rest." It is a nightly biological maintenance program where your brain reorganizes memories, regulates emotions, repairs tissues, balances hormones, strengthens immunity, and removes metabolic waste.

💤 Understanding the Sleep Cycle

A complete sleep cycle lasts about 90-120 minutes and repeats 4-6 times per night.

🌅

Stage 1 - Light Sleep

  • Lasts 1-7 minutes
  • Heart rate slows
  • Muscles relax
  • Easy to wake up
😴

Stage 2 - Stable Sleep

  • Makes up about 50% of total sleep
  • Body temperature drops
  • Brain activity slows
🔋

Stage 3 - Deep Sleep

  • Physical recovery occurs
  • Growth hormone is released
  • Immune system strengthens
  • Most difficult stage to wake from
🧠

REM Sleep - Dream Sleep

  • Brain becomes highly active
  • Memory consolidation occurs
  • Learning and creativity improve
  • Most vivid dreams happen here

💊 Medications for Sleep

Medication should generally be considered only after sleep hygiene and lifestyle improvements. Always consult a healthcare professional before using sleep medications.

Benzodiazepines for Sleep

Benzodiazepines are prescription medications that can help with insomnia, but they have significant limitations and risks.

⚠️ Important Limitations of Benzodiazepines:

  • ✘ Reduce deep sleep (critical for physical recovery)
  • ✘ Suppress REM sleep (important for memory and learning)
  • ✘ Produce dependence with regular use
  • ✘ Cause tolerance (requiring higher doses over time)
  • ✘ Alter memory formation

This is why they are usually recommended only for short-term use.

Common Benzodiazepines and Z-Drugs Prescribed for Sleep:

Meloset 3mg (Melatonin)

Primary Use: Short-term treatment of insomnia and circadian rhythm disorders.

How It Works: Contains melatonin hormone that regulates the sleep-wake cycle and resets the body's internal clock.

Key Benefits:

  • Natural hormone supplement with minimal side effects
  • Regulates sleep patterns and improves sleep quality
  • Non-habit forming and safe for short-term use
  • Ideal for jet lag and shift work sleep issues

Important Notes: Take 30-60 minutes before bedtime. May cause morning grogginess if taken too late. Not for long-term daily use without medical supervision.

Know More
Zolpidem (Ambien)

Primary Use: FDA-approved treatment for insomnia in adults.

How It Works: Slows brain activity through GABA receptors to help you fall asleep faster and stay asleep longer.

Key Benefits:

  • Reduces time to fall asleep (sleep onset)
  • Helps maintain sleep throughout the night
  • Shorter half-life than benzodiazepines
  • Available in immediate and extended-release forms

Dosage: Take one dose (5-10mg for women, 5mg for men) immediately before bed. Requires 7-8 hours of sleep time. Less sleep time can cause next-day impairment and memory problems.

Warnings: Risk of dependence with prolonged use. May cause sleepwalking, sleep driving, and unusual behaviors. Avoid with alcohol or liver problems.

Know More
Zopiclone (Imovane, Zimovane)

Primary Use: Short-term treatment of severe insomnia.

How It Works: Acts on GABA receptors to help you fall asleep faster, reduce nighttime awakenings, and increase total sleep duration.

Key Benefits:

  • Effective for sleep onset and maintenance
  • Reduces nighttime awakenings
  • Increases total sleep time
  • Well-tolerated for short-term use

Duration: Limited to 2-4 weeks to prevent dependence. Long-term use reduces effectiveness and increases withdrawal risk.

Side Effects: May cause bitter metallic taste. Can impair coordination and increase fall risk in elderly. Avoid alcohol and CNS depressants.

Know More
Eszopiclone (Lunesta)

Primary Use: Treatment of insomnia, effective for both falling asleep and staying asleep.

How It Works: Enhances GABA activity to produce sedation. Helps you fall asleep quickly and maintain sleep throughout the night.

Key Benefits:

  • Effective for sleep onset and maintenance
  • Approved for long-term use
  • Maintains effectiveness over time
  • Available in 1mg, 2mg, and 3mg strengths

Risks: Next-day drowsiness, complex sleep behaviors (sleepwalking, sleep driving), memory impairment, and potential psychological effects (anxiety, depression).

Important Notes: Take immediately before bed with 7-8 hours available. Not for severe liver disease. Risk of dependence with long-term use. Requires regular medical monitoring.

Know More

🔑 Key Takeaways

✅

Z-drugs (Zolpidem, Zaleplon) generally preserve deep sleep and REM better than benzodiazepines

⚠️

Barbiturates have the highest risk profile and are rarely used for sleep today

💡

Over-the-counter antihistamines (like diphenhydramine) have minimal effect on sleep architecture but commonly cause next-day drowsiness

🏥

Short-term use (days to weeks) is generally safer than long-term use for all sleep medications

⚠️ Critical Safety Information

Never stop benzodiazepines abruptly after regular use. This can cause withdrawal symptoms including seizures. Always taper under medical supervision.

These medications can impair driving and operating machinery. Avoid these activities until you know how the medication affects you.

🧘 Premium Breathing Exercises

Master these evidence-based breathing techniques to reduce stress, improve sleep quality, and enhance relaxation. Each exercise includes guided timing and visual cues.

4-7-8 Breathing Technique

Developed by Dr. Andrew Weil, this technique helps you relax and replenish oxygen in your body.

Ready 0:00
Inhale 4 seconds
Hold 7 seconds
Exhale 8 seconds

How to Practice:

  1. Allow your lips to part gently
  2. Exhale completely, making a breathy whoosh sound
  3. Inhale silently through nose for 4 seconds
  4. Hold breath for 7 seconds
  5. Exhale for 8 seconds with whooshing sound
  6. Repeat 4-8 times

Bhramari Pranayama (Bee Breath)

A calming technique that reduces breathing and heart rate, preparing your body for sleep.

Ready 0:00
Inhale 4 seconds
Hum 6 seconds

How to Practice:

  1. Close your eyes and breathe deeply
  2. Cover ears with your hands
  3. Place fingers above eyebrows and over eyes
  4. Apply gentle pressure on sides of nose
  5. Exhale slowly through nose with humming "Om" sound
  6. Repeat 5 times

Three-Part Breathing

A simple yet powerful technique that focuses on extending your exhale to promote relaxation.

Ready 0:00
Inhale 4 seconds
Exhale 8 seconds

How to Practice:

  1. Take a long, deep inhale
  2. Exhale fully while focusing on body sensations
  3. Slow down exhale to be twice as long as inhale
  4. Continue for 5-10 minutes

Diaphragmatic Breathing

Strengthens your diaphragm and decreases oxygen needs, promoting deeper relaxation.

Ready 0:00
Inhale 5 seconds
Exhale 7 seconds

How to Practice:

  1. Lie on back with knees bent or sit in chair
  2. Place one hand on chest, one on stomach
  3. Breathe through nose, chest stays still, stomach rises
  4. Breathe out through pursed lips
  5. Practice 10-15 minutes daily

Alternate Nostril Breathing (Nadi Shodhana)

Balances the nervous system and reduces stress through controlled nasal breathing.

Ready 0:00
Left Nostril 4 seconds
Hold 4 seconds
Right Nostril 4 seconds

How to Practice:

  1. Sit with legs crossed
  2. Close right nostril with thumb
  3. Exhale fully, then close right nostril
  4. Inhale through left nostril
  5. Open right, close left, exhale through right
  6. Continue rotation for 5 minutes

Buteyko Breathing

Helps reset your breathing rhythm and manage hyperventilation for better sleep.

Normal Breath 30 seconds
Hold As long as comfortable

How to Practice:

  1. Sit in bed with mouth gently closed
  2. Breathe through nose at natural pace for 30 seconds
  3. Take one intentional breath in and out
  4. Pinch nose closed until you need to breathe
  5. Release and take deep breath through nose
  6. Repeat 5-10 times

🍽️ Foods That Affect Sleep

✅ Foods That Improve Sleep

🦃

Turkey

Small doses of tryptophan (1g) may improve both sleep latency and sleep quality. This can be achieved by consuming approximately 300g (approximately one cup) of turkey.

🎃

Pumpkin Seeds

Approximately 200g of pumpkin seeds provides around one gram of tryptophan which helps in inducing better sleep. A study found that supplementation of 500mg of magnesium appears to improve insomnia in the elderly. That's about ½ cup of pumpkin seeds daily.

🥛

Whole Milk

Milk helps control melatonin production since it is a great source of calcium, which plays a role in the regulation of melatonin. Milk is also rich in tryptophan which has a calming effect. Tryptophan in whole milk per quart is 732 mg. According to Ayurveda, warm milk is commonly recommended as an insomnia cure.

🥄

Yogurt

Contains plenty of tryptophan and is a rich source of calcium, magnesium, and vitamins B12 and B5 which induces sleep easily. People with lactose intolerance can take yogurt. The calcium present reduces wakefulness and restlessness. Magnesium enhances melatonin secretion. Vitamin B12 prevents confusion and fatigue. Vitamin B5 helps reduce stress and anxiety.

🐟

Tuna

Along with tryptophan, tuna is high in vitamin B6, which your body needs to make melatonin and serotonin.

🌾

Millet (Ragi) + Oat Bran

Millet or ragi is a good source of tryptophan, calcium, and iron. It is beneficial in conditions of anxiety, depression, and insomnia. Oats are high in magnesium and potassium, high in tryptophan, and are a good source of carbohydrates. They induce a release of serotonin, a "feel good" hormone that decreases stress and helps in sleep.

🧀

Cheese

Cottage cheese is a perfect source of protein before bed since it contains slow-digesting casein proteins. It is also enriched with calcium and tryptophan. Calcium present in cottage cheese helps the brain use the tryptophan to manufacture sleep-triggering melatonin.

🥜

Peanuts

Another food that should be considered if there is difficulty falling asleep. Peanuts are a rich source of niacin, a nutrient that helps increase the release of serotonin in the system. Peanut stem and leaf extracts are reputed to aid sleep.

🍫

Dark Chocolate

The presence of Magnesium in dark chocolates helps the cells cope with the body's circadian rhythm which is responsible for sleeping, waking, and temperature. Chocolate counteracts the negative effects of sleep deprivation. Flavonoid-rich chocolate plays a role in counteracting the detrimental effects of sleep disorders.

🍑

Dried Prunes

Dried prunes are high in magnesium which helps in regularizing the sleep-wake cycle. Studies demonstrate that dried prunes are good in anxiety conditions.

🥝

Kiwi

Recent studies demonstrated that consuming two kiwi fruits an hour before bedtime improves sleep in both total sleep time and sleep efficiency. Kiwi is rich in tryptophan.

🥑

Avocado

Avocado has high levels of folate and potassium which helps in the production of feel-good hormones serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine and regulates sleep.

🍌

Bananas

Good source of tryptophan, potassium, magnesium, serotonin, and melatonin, which help relax muscles and promote sleep. They also contain vitamin B6, which improves sleep.

🌰

Walnuts

Research conducted by the University of Texas found that walnuts are rich in melatonin and tryptophan, helping individuals fall asleep quickly. Try snacking on a small handful about 20 minutes before bed to help you relax and reach a deeper state of restful sleep.

🥜

Almonds

Almonds are rich in B vitamins, iron, zinc, calcium, magnesium, and potassium. These nutrients help regulate brain activity, relax muscles, and increase secretion of sleep-inducing compounds such as melatonin.

🥬

Leafy Greens

Lettuce has a sedative-hypnotic property and sleep-prolonging effect due to the presence of tryptophan. The white fluid visible when cutting lettuce leaves is called lactucarium, which has relaxing and sleep-inducing properties. Leafy vegetables like Swiss chard and Spinach are rich in magnesium and calcium and so are sleep-friendly.

🍒

Tart Cherry

The juice beverage prepared from tart cherries is beneficial in enhancing sleep in insomnia conditions. Drinking a glass of tart cherry juice in the morning and evening can treat insomnia, adding nearly 90 minutes of sleep to your night. Tart cherries are a natural source of melatonin and tryptophan. The anti-inflammatory properties are responsible for their mechanism of action in insomnia conditions.

🍚

Rice

Germinated brown rice increases the sleep cycle giving deeper rest. It boosts the immune system, lowers blood pressure, and assists in the treatment of anxiety disorders. White rice also has tryptophan and a high glycemic index, which promotes sleep and significantly slashes the time it takes to fall asleep.

🍵

Relaxing Herbal Teas

Chamomile tea: Contains terpenoids and flavonoids. Drinking this tea is associated with an increase in glycine, a chemical that relaxes nerves and muscles and acts like a mild sedative.
Passionfruit Tea: Has a mild sedative effect and can help induce sleep.
Valerian Tea: The root of the valerian plant is widely used for inducing sleep and improving sleep quality. Its sedative and anxiolytic properties have been proven by numerous studies.

⏱️ Effect of Sleep Timing on Sleep

Many people focus on how many hours they sleep, but when they sleep is also extremely important.

Your body is controlled by two systems:

1. Sleep Pressure (Process S)

Builds up the longer you stay awake

2. Circadian Rhythm (Process C)

Your internal biological clock that determines when your body expects sleep and wakefulness

The highest-quality sleep occurs when these two systems are aligned.

✅ Good Alignment

10 PM to 6 AM → Better sleep quality, more deep sleep, improved alertness

❌ Misalignment

2 AM to 10 AM → Poorer sleep quality, less deep sleep, reduced alertness, worse metabolic health

⚠️ Social Jet Lag

Many people sleep early on weekdays but very late on weekends. This creates "social jet lag" similar to traveling across time zones every week.

  • Monday fatigue
  • Poor concentration
  • Difficulty falling asleep Sunday night

🧬 The Circadian Clock: Your Body's Internal Timekeeper

The Master Clock

The master clock sits in a tiny brain region called the Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN).

The circadian clock is an internal timing system that keeps track of day and night even when external cues are absent.

What Controls CLOCK and BMAL1?

1. Light Exposure

Morning sunlight is the most powerful signal for strengthening circadian rhythms. Special retinal cells (ipRGCs) containing melanopsin detect blue light (460-480 nm).

2. Sleep Timing

Studies show irregular sleep schedules weaken clock gene expression. Regular sleep strengthens BMAL1 and PER oscillation.

3. Exercise

Research indicates exercise acts as a secondary zeitgeber (time cue), activating PGC-1α and AMPK pathways that interact with clock genes.

4. SIRT1 and NAD⁺

SIRT1 regulates CLOCK-BMAL1 activity. Higher NAD⁺ availability improves SIRT1 function and strengthens circadian rhythms.

5. Time-Restricted Eating

Eating during daylight hours synchronizes liver clocks and improves glucose metabolism.

6. Avoid Artificial Light at Night

Blue light at night suppresses melatonin, shifts circadian phase, and weakens clock gene rhythms.

☀️ Light Therapy: Resetting Your Clock

How Light Reaches the Circadian Clock

Step 1

Light enters the eye and is detected by special retinal cells (ipRGCs) containing melanopsin

Step 2

Signal travels through the Retinohypothalamic Tract (RHT) to the Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN)

Step 3

The SCN adjusts the body's master clock, synchronizing sleep-wake cycles, melatonin secretion, and other rhythms

Artificial Light Color and Wavelength for Better Sleep

Not all light affects sleep equally. Your brain's sleep system is especially sensitive to certain wavelengths.

❌ Blue Light (460-490 nm) - WORST

Smartphones, tablets, computer screens, cool-white LEDs

  • Suppresses melatonin
  • Delays sleep onset
  • Increases alertness
  • Shifts circadian rhythm later

✅ Warm White (2700-3000K) - GOOD

Incandescent bulbs, warm LEDs, bedside lamps

  • Less blue light
  • Less melatonin suppression
  • More sleep-friendly

✅ Amber Light (590-620 nm) - BETTER

Amber lighting contains very little blue light

  • Minimal melanopsin activation
  • Less circadian disruption
  • Ideal for 1-2 hours before bed

✅ Red Light (620-700 nm) - BEST

Red light has the weakest effect on melanopsin

  • Lowest melatonin suppression
  • Least circadian disruption
  • Good for nighttime navigation

Best Lighting Schedule for Sleep

Morning (Within 1 Hour) ☀️ Bright natural sunlight Suppress melatonin, increase alertness
Daytime ☀️ Bright indoor/outdoor lighting Maintain alertness, reinforce daytime signal
2-3 Hours Before Bed 🌅 Warm white (2700-3000K) Reduce overhead lighting and screen brightness
1 Hour Before Bed 🕯️ Amber/dim warm (1800-2200K) Reading, relaxing, light stretching
During Sleep 🌑 Complete darkness Best for melatonin production
